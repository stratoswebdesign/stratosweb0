The process

How we get to handover

Five steps, and at the end of each you know where we are and what comes next.

01

Free discovery call

You tell us what you make, who your customers are and where sales get stuck today, and we listen. By the end of the call it’s clear what the new site needs to do.

02

Tailored proposal

You get an itemised quote: what gets built, what doesn’t, and by when. The quote also sets when to send us the product data, the references and the access details, and the deadline guarantee is tied to that.

03

Starting out

Once the deposit arrives, we schedule the project and the work begins.

04

Design

We show you the design, and you shape it. Management, sales and engineering can all have their say before we start development.

05

Build and handover

We build it, test it on every device, launch it and hand it over. If you want, we run it afterwards too, and the same person keeps looking after you.

A deadline guarantee, as long as the materials reach us on time. Three rounds of changes.

Help

Frequently asked
questions

What does it cost?

The price depends on how many languages and pages the website has, and whether it needs 3D or a configurator. After the free first conversation you get an itemised quote. The website is a one-off project fee; operation and SEO are available as a monthly partnership.

Who owns the source code and the content?

At handover you receive the source code, the content and every login.

Do you take over existing sites?

Yes. First we check what can be kept and which old pages bring in traffic, so that it isn’t lost on the new site. If your current site works well, we’ll tell you that too.

We don't have the capacity.

It doesn’t take much capacity: we carry the work. From you we need the materials and a contact person who signs off at the key points. What we need and when is set out in the quote in advance.

Our sales are personal, the website doesn't matter.

You’re right: the decision is made in the personal meeting, and a website doesn’t take that over. But before the meeting people look at your site, and often that’s what decides whether there will be a meeting at all.

Show us what you make.

Write a few sentences about what customers ask you most often. In a short, free conversation we listen, and we tell you what the new site needs to answer.
